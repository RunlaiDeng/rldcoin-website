# Rldcoin: Interstellar Peer-to-Peer Payments

Runlai Deng
dengrunlai@gmail.com
www.rldcoin.com
18 October 2026

**Abstract.** Rldcoin proposes a peer-to-peer payment system combining ordinary-user permissionless mining, local fast payment and asynchronous interstellar carriage. Users must be able to validate, construct blocks and mine without an account, stake, permit, designated pool, founder service or planetary authority. An open proof-of-work base conserves one currency; a noncustodial fast payment layer targets locally usable receipt at p95 <= 3 seconds and p99 <= 5 seconds, and at least 100,000,000 completed payments per continuous 24 hours under declared operating conditions. Every payment must owe an owner-authorized, funded and verifiably miner-directed protocol fee. Across delayed or one-way contacts, portable owner-signed intents and complete evidence can travel while the sender is offline; the recipient verifies locally and can authorize a subsequent payment. Conflicting spends and rewards from displaced histories remain reversible. Seconds-scale local usability is neither instantaneous interstellar delivery nor irreversible global settlement. This paper defines required capabilities and falsifiable acceptance gates; the targets do not establish a qualified implementation, monetary deployment or operational interstellar route.

## 1. Purpose and scope

The primary requirement is replaceability: no founder machine, server, validator identity, directory or geographic origin is required for ordinary mining and payments. If only one eligible user node remains anywhere, it must not be refused solely because other participants are absent. It can construct valid transactions and attempt proof of work using its retained, independently validated history. Its rewards and payments remain conditional on that history being selected when other histories become available.

The product goal has three jointly necessary parts. Open participation gives an ordinary person a direct path to verification and solo mining on supported general-purpose hardware. Local fast payment gives a recipient independently verified, durably retained authority to continue paying within seconds under declared communication and funding conditions, at a network target of at least one hundred million payments per day. Interstellar payment transfers owner-authorized value and its validation dependencies across real, delayed contacts without keeping the sender, founder or Earth online. Every payment carries a positive protocol fee for miners. Privileged-only mining, an unchecked instant balance or a relay that never reaches recipient usability fails the corresponding goal. All parts and their composition must be tested through ordinary commands and owner-controlled wallets.

The architectural contribution is the separation of durable owner intent, transport custody and locally executed payment state. Consensus selects money; contacts move exact bytes; a recipient chooses whether the resulting conditional position is acceptable. These roles may be performed by different devices and at different times. Neither a fast local network nor a slow stellar route changes who may authorize a payment, create a subsidy or decide that a conflicting input was spent. This is a design composition, not a claim of priority over existing proof-of-work, delay-tolerant networking or causal-transfer research.

A node cannot distinguish the last surviving participant from one isolated by a partition or an attacker. Accordingly, the protocol does not promise a fixed block time, guaranteed mining income, absolute payment finality, or preservation of all disconnected branches. An offline recipient can be named in a transaction, but has not independently verified receipt. A wallet signature cannot be produced without its owner's authority, even if only one node remains.

The revision retains the replacement of mandatory regional validator quorum and irreversible regional export/import with open mining and explicit reorganization. Its local three/five-second fast-usability target is distinct from the historical irreversible final-spendability target. The monetary cap, zero initial allocation, deterministic issuance recurrence, owner authorization, exact accounting, explicit cryptographic versions and evidence-based release requirements remain mandatory. Existing signed networks and historical results are not upgraded by publication.

The companion Survivor-1 contract fixes the base consensus bytes and monetary rules [6]. Additional interstellar requirements define ordinary participation, evidence carriage and reconsideration [7]; local fast-payment requirements define seconds-scale usability, daily capacity, per-payment miner fees and their acceptance [10]. These requirements do not silently change Survivor-1 or supply an accepted fast transaction, escrow, proof or capsule codec. Any such extension needs exact versioned rules, bounds, public vectors, independent review and authenticated adoption before use. Required behavior belongs here; implementation progress belongs in the separate implementation plan.

## 2. One currency and one selected history

A fresh genesis binds the currency identity, zero issued supply, protocol and suite versions, network kind, network discriminator, start time and initial target. The exact profile is RLD-SURVIVOR-1, specified in the companion consensus, operations and conformance documents [6]. Its published identity is a common starting point, not a permanently online issuer or permission service. Independent implementations use the same consensus domains; an implementation source hash is release evidence rather than a consensus identifier. A node verifies from genesis and does not accept balances supplied by an operator, relay or serialized cache as authority.

The baseline uses one proof-of-work history per currency. Earth, a spacecraft and another stellar system describe locations of participants, not separate issuance authorities. Any eligible miner can extend the same currency's history from any location. A partition can produce competing branches, but their rewards and balances are never added together. Replaying the selected valid branch determines issuance and spendable state. Fast-layer claims must be backed by value locked under adopted base-chain rules; claims and their backing cannot both be counted as independently spendable supply.

This choice avoids inventing new money whenever a region becomes disconnected. It also means that independently growing branches do not provide permanent regional settlement autonomy: a branch may later lose selection. Independently final regional ledgers, bridges and cross-region minting rights are outside this baseline and require a separate conservation and reorganization design before adoption.

## 3. Mining, validation and chain selection

Mining is permissionless under the adopted rules. Ordinary block validity must not require approval from the founder or a fixed signer committee. A non-mining full node still verifies and relays; running a node alone earns no subsidy. A miner earns only the subsidy and permitted fees of valid blocks retained in the selected history, subject to maturity and later reorganization. All protocol payment fees belong to eligible selected-block miners, including fees verifiably settled from the fast layer. A fast operator or prover is not a privileged base signer. Ordinary miners must independently validate adopted settlement rules within the declared hardware envelope; fast-layer funding prerequisites cannot become mining prerequisites.

Permissionless has operational meaning. A conforming user must need no registration, identity check, invitation, payment to an operator, prior RLD balance, stake, validator seat, manufacturer attestation, subscription, remote API credential or mandatory pool. The ordinary client must construct and validate its own block template and let the user choose a valid reward key. Mining can be enabled, bounded and stopped locally without surrendering a spending key. A pool or hosted service is optional; its failure cannot disable independent validation or solo work. Public rules and resource prerequisites apply equally to founder and newcomer. A neighbor's willingness to carry bytes is separate from mining eligibility; no protocol can compel an unwilling radio operator to provide a contact.

General-purpose hardware is a concrete release obligation, not an assertion that every powered device is adequate. The supported envelope must include ordinary ARM64 and x86-64 computers mining without a specialized accelerator. Publish reproducible installation, full-validation and solo-mining measurements with RAM, disk, energy, thermal behavior, verification cost and interactive usability. Users control worker and power budgets locally; those controls cannot alter the network's required work. The same exact header and work must be checked on both architectures. A release must explain what a measured work rate implies at the actual selected difficulty, including potentially negligible reward prospects.

Open entry does not give one-person-one-vote or equal income. Software cannot distinguish one person with many machines from many people. Double-SHA256 permits general-purpose computation but also specialized-hardware advantage. The baseline does not promise ASIC resistance, profitable laptop mining or equal rewards. A future CPU-oriented or memory-hard work profile must fix exact code-independent semantics, parameters, key derivation, cross-architecture vectors and hostile verification costs; its name alone is no security argument. It cannot silently reuse the current work score or upgrade existing value.

Every accepted block must satisfy its parent linkage, difficulty, proof of work, canonical encoding, size and resource bounds, owner signatures, input uniqueness, maturity and exact issuance rules. An invalid branch never wins, regardless of claimed work. For target T, each block contributes floor(2^256 / (T+1)); genesis contributes zero. A full node recomputes cumulative work from validated targets rather than trusting a peer's score. Among complete eligible valid branches it selects the greatest cumulative work; equal work selects the lexicographically smallest tip block ID. Peer count, geography and claimed uptime never confer weight. No confirmation-depth checkpoint prevents a valid deeper reorganization.

A reorganization removes the old suffix and executes the new suffix atomically. Displaced rewards cease to exist in the selected state. Displaced payments may be reconsidered only if their inputs and signatures remain valid; conflicting payments do not survive by combining balances. Wallets must report changed confirmations and conflicts. A backup, relay acknowledgment or previous local acceptance cannot override the selected ledger.

Security depends on actual work, communication and validation. A lone miner can continue computation but offers no independent resistance to a stronger competing history. A small or isolated network must disclose its exposure to withholding, eclipse, double-spend and majority-work attacks. Confirmations observed under isolation are not an estimate of unseen honest work.

## 4. Difficulty and single-node operation

A proof-of-work network permits a single miner to try; it does not guarantee that the miner can solve the current target promptly. For stable work rate H and required expected work W, expected block time is W/H, not a deadline. If H falls sharply, the next block can be much later than the nominal interval. There is no finite worst-case discovery time.

Survivor-1 fixes double-SHA256 header work, initial and maximum target 2^248-1, nominal spacing 600 seconds, and a 144-block adjustment window. At a parent height divisible by 144, the base target scales by the timestamp span of the preceding 144 mined blocks divided by 85800 seconds; the span is clamped to 21450 through 343200 seconds and the resulting target to 1 through 2^248-1. Other blocks inherit the parent's base target. A child timestamp must exceed its parent's timestamp and the median of up to eleven ancestors. Local activation defers a block more than 7200 seconds ahead of a reliable local clock; delayed old evidence has no maximum delivery age. All arithmetic and byte encodings are fixed by [6].

When the child timestamp is at least 14400 seconds after its parent, the same public consensus rule permits the maximum target. The recovery block receives only the work of that actual target; it does not inherit the harder base score or reset base difficulty. No peer-count claim, founder approval, quorum or emergency key is involved. At that maximum target a uniformly distributed header succeeds with probability 1/256 per distinct trial. This preserves a probabilistic path for a weak surviving miner, not a four-hour deadline. Repeated recovery blocks and reward maturity may still take many days. Timestamp manipulation, waiting attacks, work oscillation and withholding require independent analysis; the rule does not inherit Bitcoin's confirmation probabilities.

Single-node acceptance must therefore report eligibility, actual block discoveries, elapsed time, resource use and uncertainty. It must separately test loss of most mining capacity at a previously harder target. Passing an easy fresh-genesis example does not establish useful liveness after a production-scale collapse in work rate.

Recovery is an availability tradeoff rather than a hidden fast-payment claim. If a hard base target leaves a weak miner relying on repeated recovery gaps, 100 such gaps cover 400 timestamp-hours before reward maturity; random discovery adds further waiting. Timestamp gaps are not proof of elapsed physical time, and local future-clock allowance affects eligibility. Tests must include 99% and 99.99% work loss from a retained hard history, future timestamps, withheld recovery branches and early low-difficulty reward concentration. Zero genesis allocation does not by itself establish a fair launch. Public launch notice, exact artifact availability and the distribution of early selected rewards require separate review; there is no privileged founder mining mode.

## 5. Ownership, transfer and confirmation

A payment spends explicitly identified outputs using their owners' signatures. All amounts are checked integers; inputs equal new outputs plus explicit fees. Unknown inputs, duplicate spends, invalid signatures, overflow and immature rewards refuse. Change belongs to the authorized owner. Fees move existing value and cannot expand supply.

Wallets distinguish created, submitted, relayed, included, confirmations observed, locally spendable, conflicted and reorganized. Confirmation count belongs to an exact selected block and observed history; it is neither a delivery receipt nor irreversible finality. A recipient independently validates the payment and its inputs before choosing whether to accept its risk. An actual subsequent payment demonstrates local execution, not immunity to a later reorganization.

The candidate baseline makes a reward created at height h eligible in a spending block at height h+100, including its associated miner fees. Ordinary outputs may be spent only in a later block; same-block child transactions refuse. Reorganization can remove both reward and descendants. One strict Ed25519 owner authorizes each transfer's ordered inputs, outputs, explicit fee and height expiry. The default expiry is the maximum u128 height, avoiding an unintended short deadline for interstellar carriage. User interfaces must not turn local maturity, an acknowledgment, cached balance or connectivity indicator into a final-settlement claim.

Offline recipients can receive an output addressed to their key without signing the receipt. They cannot independently confirm it until evidence reaches their node. Losing all valid history, necessary software, keys or resources is outside the single-surviving-node premise. No surviving node may spend somebody else's output without authorization.

The ordinary asynchronous transfer and fast-layer update are separate modes. Fast-layer state changes need explicit owner authorization under their own adopted version. They cannot rewrite an existing signed ordinary intent or spend its reserved backing twice. Loss of fast liquidity or a counterparty may stop fast service while ordinary survivor validation and probabilistic mining remain available; fallback does not acquire a seconds-scale deadline.

An intent must survive a change in its carriage. The owner signs exact currency, input outpoints, ordered output owners and amounts, fee and height expiry. A relay may supply another valid inclusion history, but may not change any signed field, substitute a key or increase the fee. A payment's first submission time and signed bytes remain retained through retries. A destination address is sufficient for making the output; a return signature, account registration or reachable receiver service is not a prerequisite to signing it.

After reorganization, durable wallet and pool policy reconsider the original transaction in dependency order. If its exact inputs still exist, belong to its signer, are mature and unspent, and its original expiry permits inclusion, the same signature may be offered again. Dependencies between ordinary outputs require successive blocks under the parent-only rule. Reconsideration must not double-reserve funds or silently first-sign a replacement. A missing dependency waits; an input spent elsewhere is conflicted; an expired intent is expired; a reward removed with its branch and every dependent payment remain unsupported by that state. Retain the original intent and its cause, even when it cannot be reoffered. Inclusion still depends on valid inputs, capacity, fees and miners' choices.

For example, Alice's input already exists in the common history before a long partition. Her unchanged signed payment to Bob can be reoffered after reconnection if that exact input survives unspent. If Alice instead spends a reward created only on the losing branch, neither Bob's receipt nor his subsequent signature can recreate that reward. Non-conflicting owner intentions can be retained; non-conflicting intentions are not necessarily backed by surviving money. This distinction prevents evidence preservation from becoming compensation issuance.

## 5A. Local fast payments and daily capacity

The required architecture separates the open proof-of-work base, a noncustodial fast payment layer and asynchronous interstellar carriage. A funded payment channel is one candidate method: participants can authorize multiple balance updates against locked value and settle under base-chain rules [11]. A channel, batch or cryptographic proof is not itself a complete Rldcoin design. The adopted extension must specify exact fund ownership, canonical state, signatures, data availability, update ordering, disputes, unilateral exit, fees, limits and reorganization behavior [10]. A server credit or committee acknowledgment cannot substitute for these rules.

For every predeclared normal local communication/load cohort, usable receipt must meet p95 <= 3 seconds and p99 <= 5 seconds. The original clock starts with payer authorization/submission, before batching, routing or retries. It stops only after the recipient independently verifies and durably retains an enforceable, funded fast state permitting onward payment. Actual recipient-authorized onward payments must complete in each declared composition cohort. A display animation, signature receipt or cached balance is insufficient. Base-chain inclusion, withdrawal and final settlement are separate observations, with their own waiting and reorganization exposure.

Predeclare hardware, geography, round-trip delay, loss, uptime, amounts, routes, funding, history, load and clock uncertainty. First use, funding, insufficient liquidity, disconnected recipients and forced exit need separate named cohorts and honest pending/refusal behavior. Every valid submission within a normal cohort remains in the original denominator, including capacity refusal, retries, failures and pending operations. Uncompleted work has infinite completion latency for percentiles; counts, causes, completion ratio and pending ages remain separately visible. Outcomes cannot be used to narrow a cohort or reset its clock.

The capacity target is at least 100,000,000 distinct, valid, completed user payments in one continuous 86,400-second wall-clock interval, averaging approximately 1,157.41 payments per second. The same campaign must meet the local latency targets with actual signatures, independently verified funded states, durable custody and per-payment miner-fee entitlements. Predeclare peak rates and burst lengths, user/payment distributions, amounts, route lengths, liquidity movement, independent operators and CPU/RAM/disk/bandwidth budgets. Count each logical payment once; submissions, duplicate retries, relay copies and batch roots are not additional payments. Report total demand, failures, backlog and drain time. A short sample extrapolated to a day cannot establish this target.

The base must also support the declared fast system's funding, liquidity maintenance, settlement and hostile mass-exit demand. Survivor-1 admits at most 128 transfers and 262,144 bytes per block with nominal 600-second spacing: at full count and nominal spacing, only 18,432 transfers per day, without a guaranteed service rate [6]. That bound establishes neither daily fast-payment capacity nor safe exit capacity. Before adopting a fast scale, specify and qualify safe admission limits, dispute windows, base capacity and inclusion/security assumptions on ordinary hardware. An overloaded exit path fails the advertised scale. Merely shortening block spacing or increasing limits is not a security or decentralization argument.

Users retain their keys and must have a specified unilateral base-chain recovery path under explicit communication and inclusion assumptions. Old-state publication, unavailable data/operators, monitoring failures, censorship, crashes, rollback and depleted liquidity require concrete behavior. Channel monitoring and dispute mechanisms illustrate additional assumptions, not universal safety during indefinite disconnection [12]. Deep base-chain reorganization can invalidate locked backing, fast claims and their fees. Local fast usability must never be labeled absolute finality. Exact extension versions and independent safety/conservation review precede adoption.

## 6. Supply and issuance

The maximum supply is 100,000,000,000 RLD, exactly C = 10^35 runlai, with 1 RLD = 10^24 runlai. Genesis height 0 issues nothing and allocates nothing. The reserve belongs to the currency's deterministic schedule, not to the founder or to a planet. Any eligible miner may compete for the next selected block's reward; regional labels grant no extra reserve.

For height h >= 1, let L = 200000, era j = floor((h-1)/L), and slot k = (h-1) mod L. At era start R_j is the remaining reserve. If R_j >= 2, B_j = floor(R_j/2); otherwise B_j = R_j. Let q_j = floor(B_j/L) and m_j = B_j mod L. The reward is q_j + 1 runlai when k < m_j, otherwise q_j. A completed era leaves R_(j+1) = R_j - B_j. All arithmetic is checked integers. An incomplete era issues only rewards from its selected blocks. Reorganization recomputes the reserve from the replacement history; competing branches never accumulate independent supply.

```text
EraBudget(R):
  require 0 <= R <= 10^35
  return R if R < 2 else floor(R / 2)
Reward(R, k):
  require 0 <= k < 200000
  B = EraBudget(R)
  q, m = divmod(B, 200000)
  return q + (1 if k < m else 0)
```

Boundary vectors retain the original recurrence. With R_0 = 10^35, heights 1 through 200000 each issue 250000 RLD; height 200001 issues 125000 RLD. For R = 3, 2 or 1, slot 0 issues one runlai and the remaining slots issue zero; the next reserves are 2, 1 and 0. For R = 0 all rewards are zero. For R = 400003, slot 0 issues two runlai and every other slot issues one. The final one-runlai reserve is released once. No calendar, service receipt or compensation claim creates additional issuance.

For each fully replayed selected history, cumulative issuance equals the sum of all unspent outputs, including immature rewards, and never exceeds C. Fees are counted once through their recipient outputs. No baseline bridge reserve, imported duplicate balance or off-ledger compensation is allowed. Any future escrow extension must define mutually exclusive accounting and reorganization behavior. A supply cap does not guarantee price, liquidity, fair distribution or a funded long-term security budget.

## 6A. A miner-directed protocol fee on every payment

Each conforming ordinary or fast payment must owe a positive protocol fee in runlai, explicitly bound by owner authorization to the currency, payment, amount and applicable rules. All such fees belong to miners rather than a founder, treasury or fast-layer operator. This does not distribute money equally to all nodes: the adopted rules must identify the eligible selected-block miner or miners and their exact allocation. Ordinary C04/C08 fees already join the selected block's reward; any fast-layer mechanism must make its miner fee equally enforceable under its own specified rules [10].

Fast completion must reserve existing value and create a verifiable miner-fee entitlement. Specify canonical evidence, amount, allocation, settlement trigger and deadline/exit path. Batched settlement must natively verify each conforming payment's fee and the exact aggregate; an unsigned count, final balance or batch hash alone cannot prove completeness. Test omitted fees, unauthorized changes, duplicate charging/claims and attempts to erase accrued fees through cooperative close, dispute or withdrawal. Fees cannot be funded from unissued reserve, fabricated balances or compensation issuance.

Accrual, actual inclusion and mature miner receipt are separate states. Reserved or accrued fees are not already paid to a miner. Measure actual settlement, failures and delay; settled miner fees retain 100-block reward maturity and reverse with losing history. No fixed payout deadline follows when no future block is discovered. Independently review whether miners, liquidity and routing services can sustain the declared load. Ordinary channel networks can charge routing fees to forwarding nodes [13]; adopting channels does not automatically satisfy miner-directed per-payment fees. No mandatory operator fee may relabel or divert the required protocol fee. Separately owner-authorized optional services must disclose their distinct price and total cost.

## 7. Interstellar communication and payment

The transport target is authenticated, bounded store-and-forward. Nodes learn reachable neighbors, retain admitted transactions and evidence durably, and forward when a contact exists. Normal startup should support relay without a mandatory Earth directory. Bootstrap requires some prior trusted currency information and an actual contact or carried archive; discovery cannot manufacture connectivity.

The local fast layer does not shorten a physical interstellar path. Stellar localities may offer fast service only within separately declared communication, backing and dispute envelopes, conditional on selected history; they gain no independent mint or permanent settlement authority. Source authorization, remote usable arrival, return acknowledgment and base settlement retain separate clocks. A dispute needing return traffic or a bounded monitoring window cannot be presumed safe on a one-way or indefinitely disconnected leg. Unsupported fast paths must refuse without removing ordinary asynchronous payment carriage.

A portable payment capsule is a logical collection of an exact signed transaction, its currency identity, a bounded manifest of validation dependencies and observations of particular inclusion histories. It is not a new coin, a destination mint or an authority certificate. The owner signature remains unchanged when later evidence is attached. The transport authenticates origin, destination and exact carried bytes; the node executes the transaction under consensus. Before an implementation offers a capsule codec, that codec must define canonical bytes, versions, manifest ordering, complete-byte commitments, chunk reassembly, size and quota bounds, and cross-implementation negative vectors. Calling a message a capsule grants no new accepted wire format.

Evidence resolves to complete native-validated history from the pinned genesis or an exact predecessor already fully executed by the recipient under the identical rules. A manifest, state root, work claim, header chain or compressed representation cannot initialize a trusted balance. Deduplication may share only exact authenticated bytes while retaining their necessary dependencies. Missing material is requested or carried later and remains unresolved. Admission bounds and interrupted assembly must preserve earlier admitted obligations. A sender cannot hide the full-history cost by reporting only a small transaction or manifest.

Carriage is contact-first rather than request-response-first. Ordinary nodes must proactively offer missing history and payment bytes over a configured one-way path even if no reverse request or receipt can return. Evidence is offered dependency-first, in bounded fair batches, without letting one indefinitely missing ancestor or one large payment starve all unrelated admitted work. The sender may leave after durable custody is accepted by a willing relay; this transfers a byte-retention obligation, not a guarantee of destination delivery or monetary settlement. A destination receipt may travel later by another path. Permanent receipt loss remains unknown to the sender and must not block otherwise valid recipient execution.

A relay identity, route advertisement, packet digest or custody receipt grants no ownership or issuance authority. Survivor-1 defines signed origin packets, pinned neighbor identities, interactive challenge-bound delivery, offline one-way frames and signed destination-receipt return packets. Ground interactive links use pinned TLS 1.3 without downgrade. Origin identity, exact destination and bytes remain authenticated across forwarding; there is no claim of a complete signed physical path. Duplicate packets require exact-byte checks and durable custody before acknowledgment. Default carriage admission is 4096 packets and 256 MiB, separate from ledger history growth. Capacity refusal preserves existing obligations. A hop receipt alone cannot delete the last addressed copy; a valid destination receipt or explicit withdrawal is required. Message expiry never refunds a payment.

Neighbor discovery may describe signed candidate routes, but an advertisement must not choose a node's trusted genesis, signing authority, connection endpoint or certificate pin. Admission to a privately operated link is explicit and locally controlled; a missing pin or unreachable first contact is a communication limitation, not loss of the right to mine. Mobile carriers, removable archives and future radio or optical adapters must preserve exact-byte authentication, quotas and custody across changes of medium. BPv7 or BPSec interoperability requires a specified adapter and its own vectors; mentioning those standards is not implementation or route qualification [3,8].

Cross-stellar payment in the baseline means carrying a transaction and enough authenticated history for the recipient to validate it on the same currency. It is not a separate destination mint. The receiving node must reconcile the received branch with its own valid history before reporting confirmations or spendability. Missing ancestors remain unresolved. An isolated sender's claim of greatest work is not proof that no stronger history exists elsewhere.

The earliest independent receipt is bounded by actual propagation and contact availability; prolonged or permanent disconnection can make delivery indefinitely pending. NASA's delay-tolerant networking and Bundle Protocol provide relevant transport techniques [2,3], but do not prove monetary finality, key longevity or route availability. Physical contacts, operators and storage must be qualified separately for each advertised route.

Each advertised route needs a predeclared useful operating envelope: directed contacts and outages, payload capacity, storage, replay CPU, supported history length and divergence, and the maximum acceptable pending exposure chosen by the user. A fresh destination may need the whole relevant chain; evidence growth can outrun contact capacity. Measure total dependency bytes, retries, backlog and actual onward payment, including failures. A route with delivered packets but no independently spendable recipient output has not demonstrated payment usefulness. Earth-to-Mars, Earth-to-nearest-star and a spacecraft-local network require separate envelopes; topology drawings are neither physical links nor evidence of viable monetary settlement.

The payment interface must bind each observation to an exact transaction and selected tip, observation time, inclusion position, validated work after inclusion, known competing validated heads, missing history and locally observed contact history. Unseen competing work is UNKNOWN. Contact age, signed peer status and confirmation count cannot prove global freshness or yield a justified numeric attack probability by themselves. A recipient may set a local amount or exposure limit and decline a conditional position. Such a policy changes that user's acceptance, not issuance, mining eligibility or consensus validity. A receipt received today still describes the exact historical event it authenticates.

The ordinary interstellar flow is deliberately asymmetric:

```text
Owner signs exact intent and retains its reservation
  -> willing relays carry intent and complete dependencies
  -> recipient validates history and selects a local branch
  -> recipient sees conditional balance and its evidence gaps
  -> recipient signs a real onward payment when locally eligible
Return receipts may follow later; silence never credits or refunds.
```

Long interstellar partitions can create deep competing histories. When contact resumes, cumulative-work selection may displace years of a weaker branch. The baseline therefore supports delayed transport and conditional payment verification, not guaranteed preservation of autonomous stellar economies. Useful interstellar settlement under realistic work distributions and latency remains an open acceptance problem. Merely increasing confirmation counts cannot remove this limitation.

## 8. Keys, archives and upgrades

Private keys remain with their owners. The candidate defines native encrypted wallet key files using fixed scrypt parameters and AES-256-GCM; a key file alone is not a complete wallet backup. Reviewed intent and input reservations are durable before signature release; exact retries recover the original signed transfer rather than first-signing again. Reorganization restores unresolved reservations when inputs reappear. Restore must validate full history and retained operations; an old backup cannot prove its own freshness. Without independently retained freshness evidence, the wallet discloses FRESHNESS_UNKNOWN and requires explicit local risk acknowledgment before signing; mining with an existing public reward key remains possible. Direct-key loss has no protocol reset or administrator compensation. Cross-device custody and hardware failure still require independent testing.

A usable survivor needs recoverable data. Nodes must specify archive retention, resource ceilings, pruning dependencies, full replay and recovery behavior. A digest identifies bytes; it cannot substitute for execution or recreate missing evidence. Test history beyond the first 200000-block issuance era, long partitions and deep reorganization within explicitly declared storage and CPU bounds. If an implementation cannot safely reconcile a history within those bounds it must refuse the affected operation and disclose the limitation, not fabricate acceptance.

Protocol, work algorithm, signature suite, hash and key-era identifiers must be explicit. Upgrades require public rules, compatible history interpretation and independently verifiable adoption. Unknown or expired cryptographic assumptions cannot authorize new acceptance. Post-quantum signatures, renewal and long-delayed revocation remain separate engineering and review obligations; their presence in a component does not qualify monetary use.

No finite cryptographic or storage test establishes a hundred-million-year guarantee. Long continuity requires funded maintenance, generational succession, media refresh, authenticated renewal and finite periods of requalification. Loss beyond recoverable evidence remains loss, not permission to issue replacement currency.

## 9. Threats and limitations

The principal threats are dominant or withheld work, network eclipse and partition, double spending, timestamp and difficulty manipulation, invalid-history flooding, storage exhaustion, key compromise, rollback, software supply-chain compromise and misleading payment displays. Every release must state which assumptions and attack budgets its evidence covers. Node count is not a measure of independent work or control.

Protecting ordinary participation also requires bounded work before expensive authentication, fair local service, disk admission, and isolation of contact pressure from local mining and owner review. These are availability defenses rather than membership gates. Every complete accepted transaction, block and received envelope still needs its required verification. A process-local cache, reputation score, puzzle or rate limit must not turn unchecked evidence into value or make an operator's identity the consensus admission rule. Authenticated peers can still send costly valid material; the declared hardware envelope must include hostile admitted load.

An attacker may reorganize payments using stronger valid work; it cannot make an invalid signature or excessive subsidy valid. Economic loss can still occur even when all protocol rules execute correctly. Risk grows when a recipient cannot compare histories, when work is concentrated, or when a payment is accepted before competing work can propagate. Privacy is not anonymity: transaction graphs, contact metadata and addresses can reveal relationships.

The design cannot simultaneously provide unrestricted progress in every isolated partition, one consistent spend history and irrevocable acceptance of conflicting payments. This revision chooses continued local attempts and explicit reorganization. It does not silently inherit the older committee-finality or regional export/import guarantees. The required fast layer needs separately reviewed, versioned rules and cannot be advertised as an existing Survivor-1 capability. Bridges and independent regional settlement remain outside the baseline. Neither a fast receipt nor a channel commitment protects value whose base backing disappears.

The smallest counterexample uses one software-owned input copied before spending. A malicious owner signs one payment to Bob and another to Carol, whose isolated nodes cannot communicate. If both must accept irrevocably using only their local evidence, both can accept the same predecessor. Hash chains, local counters, signatures or extra work do not prove that the other copy never existed. Later conflict detection, a deterministic winner or insurance cannot make both earlier receipts irrevocable and conserved. The exclusive-rights research contract makes the assumptions and conditional alternatives explicit [9].

Causal ledgers and disjoint issuance rights remain promising research directions for preserving independent stellar economies. They require a concrete non-equivocation mechanism, open and Sybil-resistant allocation of every issuance slot, authority succession, complete causal evidence and exact conservation. An ideal single writer is an assumption, not a solution for copyable ordinary machines. An intersecting quorum can exclude conflicts under authenticated membership and honest-lock assumptions but may be unavailable to a lone survivor. Qualified non-rollbackable hardware adds device trust and replacement obligations. A DAG alone neither prevents duplicate spending nor assigns the unchanged reserve. No such alternative can replace the baseline by relabeling its assumptions or weakening the cap.

## 10. Acceptance and release

G1 - Open participation and survivor operation. An independent user installs the published software, validates genesis/history, creates a wallet and mines without a founder service or fixed signer approval. Shut down the founder and every other fixture node; retain the survivor's original history and target. Demonstrate block discovery, mature reward spending, receipt and an actual second owner-authorized payment. Report failures, waiting times and the exact work assumptions. Repeat with different surviving participants and with a sharp loss of mining capacity.

G2 - Partition and convergence. Separate miners and owners, submit conflicting spends, extend both branches and reconnect. Verify exact cumulative-work selection, equal-work behavior, displaced rewards, transaction reconsideration and atomic cold replay. No invalid history wins and no branch balances are combined. Include eclipse, withholding, timestamps, oscillating work, invalid proofs and resource exhaustion.

G3 - Money and wallets. Verify the cap, zero allocation, all issuance boundary vectors, 100-block reward maturity, fees, checked arithmetic, signatures, backups, reorganization displays and cross-device recovery. Measure submission, inclusion, observed confirmations and recipient acceptance separately. Retain every valid submission and original clock; no success-only performance statistics.

G4 - Delayed transport. Use ordinary nodes and actual authenticated contacts to carry complete evidence through interruption, duplication, reordering, corruption, limited capacity and restart. Distinguish transport custody from locally accepted money. Test long logical delays and deep divergent histories; simulations do not qualify physical routes or elapsed cryptographic lifetime. Confirm that an offline-recipient output is never reported as independently received before verification.

G5 - Independent security and durability. Obtain independent protocol and implementation review, independent validation implementations and operators, reproducible builds, declared work/security budgets, full-history recovery beyond an issuance era, cryptographic upgrade and long-term archive plans. Resolve release-blocking issues; preserve negative evidence and exact source/profile bindings. A single developer controlling multiple keys is not independent qualification.

G6 - Authenticated release. Publish the executable consensus profile, adoption rules, audited artifacts, supported hardware/resource envelope and operating limits. Only then use a new signed zero-allocation genesis with fresh non-test custody and explicit release authorization. Test balances, test keys and old abandoned networks never migrate. Verify actual distributed packages and services. This publication authorizes no mainnet, real-money activity or automatic upgrade.

G7 - Ordinary-user mining. On predeclared ordinary ARM64 and x86-64 computers, an independent user builds or verifies the package, constructs a local template, chooses a reward key and solo mines with founder, account, hosted API and pool services absent. No prior balance, stake, allowlist or vendor attestation is supplied. Measure valid work, verification cost, CPU/RAM/disk, energy/thermal effects and reward uncertainty under the actual target; include admitted hostile contact load. Test local stop/resume, retained hard-history collapse and early reward concentration. All predeclared devices, attempts, failures and original clocks remain in the denominator. Proof that a binary starts is not evidence of usable participation or profitability.

G8 - Causal payment usefulness. A sender authorizes one exact intent, hands it to willing relays and goes offline. Over a directed path with no return traffic, carry a prefix longer than one batch, the payment and complete dependencies. The destination independently selects and cold-replays its history, observes local eligibility and makes an actual owner-authorized onward payment. Reconnect unequal-work partitions and test surviving common-prefix inputs, removed rewards, conflicting spends, original expiry and chained descendants. Verify exact-signature reconsideration, unchanged reservations and no duplicate credits. Report dependency bytes, waiting/backlog, all pending and failed payments, selected and displaced value, and the user's predeclared acceptance/exposure policy. Merely delivering a capsule, preserving a signature or passing a small easy-work fixture cannot establish useful interstellar settlement.

G9 - Local seconds-scale usable receipt. In every predeclared normal local cohort, measure original payer authorization/submission through independent, durable, funded recipient usability at p95 <= 3 seconds and p99 <= 5 seconds. Complete actual owner-authorized onward payments. Include retries, capacity refusals, failures and pending ages; unfinished operations have infinite completion latency. Separate funding, first use, loss of liquidity, disconnection, base settlement and forced exit. Test enforceable unilateral recovery, old-state attacks, missing data, custody failure and deep reorganization. A fast display or acknowledgment does not satisfy this gate.

G10 - One hundred million daily payments. Complete at least 100,000,000 distinct valid user payments in one continuous 86,400-second wall-clock interval with actual signatures, funded durable states, per-payment miner fees and the G9 latency conditions. Predeclare peaks, users, amounts, routes, liquidity, hardware/history and resource budgets; retain all offered demand, failures, pending work and backlog. Verify base funding/settlement and hostile mass-exit capacity at the claimed scale, and independently reproduce the campaign. Duplicate messages, bare counters and extrapolated short tests do not qualify.

G11 - Enforceable miner fees on every payment. Verify a positive owner-authorized protocol fee and reserved existing backing for every completed ordinary/fast payment. Independently validate per-payment completeness, exact batch totals and specified selected-miner allocation. Refuse fee omission/substitution, erased accrual, duplicate claims and unauthorized withdrawals; demonstrate actual base-chain settlement, 100-block reward maturity, conservation and reorg handling. Disclose routing/liquidity/proving/storage funding. An operator's fee promise or unpaid aggregate is insufficient.

The complete Survivor-1 candidate contract defines canonical bytes, work and recovery, accounting, branch selection, durable transition behavior, wallet state and delayed transport [6]. G1 maps to T06-T07; G2 to T05/T08-T10; G3 to T01-T04/T11-T13/T17/T22; G4 to T14-T16/T21; G5 to T18-T20 and independent review; G6 to authenticated adoption and release. D01-D06 and D-T01-D-T08 bind G7/G8 [7]. F01-F06 and F-T01-F-T08 bind G9-G11 and their composition with G1-G8 [10]. All G1-G11 are mandatory product gates; the daily campaign must compose seconds-scale usable receipt, genuine funded payments and miner fees rather than combine unrelated demonstrations. Existing T01-T22 remain mandatory; the I/S/R/A-G/N/P mapping retains its explicit historical dispositions. A release may claim only actually established hardware, security and route envelopes. No paper, simulation or collection of local passes substitutes for independent qualification or establishes irreversible global settlement.
